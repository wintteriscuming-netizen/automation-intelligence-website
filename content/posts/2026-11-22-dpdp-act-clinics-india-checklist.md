Your clinic collects names, phone numbers and health details every day: on appointment forms, WhatsApp chats, your website and booking software. India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) law changes how that data must be handled, and the main obligations are coming in 2027. This is general information, not legal advice.

## The dates that matter

The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with a phased rollout. Provisions about the Data Protection Board took effect immediately, rules for Consent Managers follow from 13 November 2026, and the main compliance obligations apply 18 months after notification, in mid-May 2027 ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/dpdp-act-2023-simple-guide-businesses-india.html), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1830402/dpdp-act-and-rules-2025-the-2026-compliance-milestones-businesses-cant-afford-to-miss)). Reports also note that serious failures, such as weak security safeguards, can attract penalties of up to ₹250 crore. Most clinics don't need to panic, but they should use the months before May 2027 to prepare.

## An 8-step clinic checklist

1. **Map your data.** List what you collect (names, numbers, symptoms, reports), where it sits (paper, software, WhatsApp, email) and who can see it.
2. **Collect only what you need.** Remove form fields you don't use.
3. **Write a plain-language privacy notice.** Say what you collect, why and how a patient can raise a concern. Put it on your website forms and booking pages.
4. **Record consent.** For WhatsApp reminders and marketing messages, keep a record of when and how the patient agreed.
5. **Take extra care with children's data.** Plan how you will get consent from a parent or guardian.
6. **Secure the data.** Use strong passwords, restrict access by role, back up regularly and remove access when staff leave.
7. **Review your vendors.** Check how your booking software, WhatsApp provider and website host protect and store patient data.
8. **Prepare a breach plan.** Decide who does what if data leaks, and how patients and authorities would be informed.

## Where marketing fits in

Your marketing tools collect data too: website forms, tracking tools, WhatsApp chatbots and review requests. Make sure each one has clear consent wording, a way to opt out and a record of agreement. Never use patient details for promotions they didn't agree to.
